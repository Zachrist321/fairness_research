Title: The Joint Effect of Prompt Specificity and Inference Hyperparameters on Stereotype Generation in Open-Source Large Language Models

Authors: Zerihun Ayele Chala (corresponding), Beakal Gizachew Assefa, Elefelious Getachew Belay, Tensae Aschalew Andualem
Affiliation: School of IT and Engineering, Addis Ababa University

We thank the Program Committee and Reviewer 1 for the constructive comments. Below we respond to each major comment and note where the camera-ready paper was revised.


Comment 1. Only three relatively small models are considered; the narrow selection limits the generalizability of the findings and should be broadened or justified.

Response: We agree that restricting the study to mid-size models limits generalizability. We retain the three open instruction-tuned models (Llama 3.1 8B, Mistral 7B, Qwen 2.5 7B) because the treatment of interest is temperature and top-p, which must be set and verified directly at generation time. Closed APIs often hide or limit those settings. Keeping models in the 7B-8B range also made the full factorial design feasible (prompt tiers, four decoding conditions, three seeds, four demographic dimensions). In the camera-ready paper we justify this choice in Model Selection and state clearly in Limitations that the findings are scoped to mid-size open models and may not hold for larger open models or proprietary systems. Extending the design to larger models is left as future work.


Comment 2. The rationale for the chosen parameter settings, such as temperature, is not explained and should be stated.

Response: We agree and have expanded the Hyperparameter Conditions section. The four settings were chosen to cover low and high randomness without a full temperature x top-p grid:
- T=0.3, top-p=1.0: near-deterministic sampling
- T=1.0, top-p=0.3: narrow token pool
- T=1.0, top-p=0.9: common open-ended setting
- T=1.5, top-p=1.0: higher chance of low-probability tokens
The camera-ready text states this rationale explicitly and retains the condition table. A separate gender temperature supplement remains a sensitivity analysis only.


Comment 3. The conclusion emphasizes that prompt design and model choice matter more than hyperparameters, but this claim requires stronger evidence than the current results provide.

Response: We agree. The original comparative ranking overstated what the primary tests support. The camera-ready paper states that the primary hypothesis (tier-by-condition interaction) was not supported. We removed absolute ranking language from the abstract, discussion, and conclusion. Prompt-tier and model differences are reported as descriptive results within this study, not as a general claim that they always matter more than hyperparameters.


We hope these revisions address the reviewer concerns.

Respectfully,
Zerihun Ayele Chala
